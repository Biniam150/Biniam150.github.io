Biniam Abrha Tsegay

I am a PhD Candidate at the School of Engineering, University of British Columbia Okanagan, advised by Dr. Nicolás M. Peleato.

I work at the intersection of machine learning, generative AI, and water infrastructure. My research focuses on improving water demand forecasting and water quality modelling in distribution systems — helping utilities make better decisions under uncertainty using data-driven methods.

I am open to collaborations, internships, and industry or research opportunities in water technology and applied machine learning.

Under Review
Forecasting-Guided Generative AI for Improved Water Demand Forecasting Across Aggregation Scales
Biniam Abrha Tsegay, Nicolás M. Peleato
Water Research(AI for SWM)· 2026
Code
Proposes CFT-VAE, a conditional variational autoencoder that generates synthetic smart meter data aligned with forecasting objectives. Evaluated across six forecasting models and multiple aggregation levels, achieving up to 65% improvement in forecasting accuracy and statistically significant gains in 23 of 24 model-aggregation comparisons.
Published · Water Research (IF 11.4)
Enhancing Long-Term Water Quality Modeling by Addressing Base Demand, Demand Patterns, and Temperature Uncertainty Using Unsupervised Machine Learning Techniques
Biniam Abrha Tsegay, Nicolás M. Peleato
Water Research, Vol. 268, January 2025
Paper Code
Introduces a three-module unsupervised framework using Gaussian Mixture Models to represent uncertainty in water demand for EPANET/WNTR simulations. Allows operators to use simple linguistic inputs like "high demand" instead of precise numerical values, achieving Jensen-Shannon Divergence below 0.008 across all demand clusters.
Published · Water (MDPI)
Analytical Solutions to Conservative and Non-Conservative Water Quality Constituents in Water Distribution System Storage Tanks
Biniam Abrha, Avi Ostfeld
Water, Vol. 13, No. 24, December 2021
Paper
Develops analytical solutions for modeling water quality in distribution system storage tanks using compartment models for both conservative and non-conservative substances, validated against numerical methods and field data.

Additional projects in progress on agent-based modeling for human–AI decision support and reinforcement learning for water distribution system control.
